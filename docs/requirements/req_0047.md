# Conflicting Generations
## Context
<!-- The situation that makes this requirement necessary, e.g. a user need, a law or standard, or a constraint from another system. -->
A mistake in the hand-written Relations File, e.g. a single swapped Relation Type, can make a Person their own ancestor, or make one Parent of a Child an ancestor of the other. Then no assignment of Generations meets the rules. The original implementation does not detect this and recurses until it runs out of stack. Resolving such a conflict is a stretch goal.

## Requirement Statement
<!-- A bland statement of the requirement using the [RFC 2119](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc2119) standardized vocabulary. The ruling SHALL be stated in an assertive voice. It SHALL NOT contain definitions or links, not even to other REQs; terms are defined in the glossary. -->
If the Library cannot assign Generations as required when it creates a Context, it SHALL NOT create the Context, SHALL tell the Application that the Context could not be created, SHALL report the Relations involved to the Trace, and SHALL release all memory it allocated while trying.

## Justification
<!-- A sound reasoning why this requirement exists and not another one. Any revision of the REQ MUST take the justification into serious consideration. -->
A Context with wrong Generations would show a wrong Family Tree without any sign that something is wrong. Refusing it makes the mistake visible at once, and the reported Relations tell the data collector where to look. Such mistakes are expected to be rare.
