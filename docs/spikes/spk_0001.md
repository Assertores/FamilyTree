# Layout of the Family Tree
<!-- State: In Progress (the spike is running, and this record is filled as it goes), Concluded (the question is answered), Abandoned (stopped without an answer, e.g. because the time box ran out, with the reason in the Findings). -->
## Question
<!-- What the spike has to find out, with the IDs of the open questions it investigates. -->
How does the Browsing Application place the boxes of the Persons within their rows, so that Families stay whole, Parents stand above their Children, Partners stand next to each other, and few lines cross? The rows themselves are given by the Generations. This investigates [OQ 0020](../open_questions.md).

## Approach
<!-- What is tried, the time box, the versions of the tools and libraries involved, and where the code lives: `spikes/spk_<number>/`. -->
A prototype in C++ with Qt 6.12, using `QGraphicsScene` and `QGraphicsView`, in `spikes/spk_0001/`. The input is the example data of the original implementation (18 Persons), and a made-up family of about 150 Persons with remarriages and Partners from different branches. Two layouts are tried:
- a simple layout: place every Family below its Parents, and center the Parents above their Children, row by row from the top;
- a layered graph layout in the style of Sugiyama, with every Family as an extra node between Parents and Children, and crossings reduced by ordering each row by the average position of its neighbours.

Time box: two days.
